Content patterns

The 127 pages fall into a handful of recurring types, often duplicated across regional WordPress sites or legacy .htm pages:

  1. Regional homepages (8) — Intro to Nose to Nose for UK, Germany, Italy, Poland, North America, plus legacy international/Italian sites.
  2. Training methodology (19) — Approach, principles, progression, course levels — often repeated per region and language.
  3. Course dates and archives (24) — Upcoming calendars and long chronological archives of past workshops.
  4. Team and people (7) — Facilitator profiles, previous associates, memorial pages.
  5. Articles and writing (15) — Essays on clowning philosophy, applied clowning, and press coverage.
  6. Applied/corporate clowning (11) — Social clowning, in-house workshops, burnout prevention, teacher development.
  7. Practical/transactional (9) — Booking instructions, contact pages, confirmation pages, legal imprint.
  8. Site infrastructure (25) — WordPress placeholders (tags, categories, locations, my-bookings), sitemap, regional link pages.

Main points the content is making

Philosophy of facilitation

  • Facilitators stay true to clowning by following each person’s creative process, not imposing a fixed technique.
  • The work nurtures emotional expression and imagination, and helps each participant find their own clown — unique to them and rooted in their relationship with the audience.
  • Focusing on the individual is presented as the heart of clowning: a search for authenticity in both personal and social life.

Who it’s for and why people return

  • The approach is framed as relevant to personal growth (“Clown Within”) and social mediation (Court Jester Group).
  • Workshops welcome people of all ages and backgrounds, with or without theatre experience.
  • Many participants return because the freedom and playfulness of the work becomes essential to them.

What happens in a workshop

  • Groups are capped at 12 participants.
  • Sessions use warm-up games, vocal and physical exercises, then stage improvisations (solo or with others).
  • After each improvisation, the group discusses what worked — to draw out the positive, distinctive qualities of each person’s clown.
  • This happens within clear rules intended to create a safe, respectful, protective environment.

The “inner clown”

  • The red nose is described as “the smallest mask in the world” and “the mask that unmasks.”
  • Clown work rediscovers openness, spontaneity, and play.
  • Imagination is encouraged while staying grounded; the inner clown emerges through improvisation and welcoming whatever arises.

Eight committed teaching principles (On principles pages and in full on the German page.)

  • Small groups — max 12, so everyone gets solo/pair time, individual coaching, and no competition for space.
  • Person-centred approach — authenticity and inner experience matter more than acquiring external skills.
  • Structured, level-appropriate teaching — groups formed by experience; curriculum introduced clearly; team meets regularly to refine methods.
  • Committed guidance and support — facilitators accompany participants gently through difficulty and foster collaborative enquiry.
  • Positive regard — feedback highlights what is working; a supportive environment is seen as essential to clowning courage.
  • Learners as their own teachers — reflection after exercises, games, and improvisations is treated as the most important part of learning.
  • Collaborative research — many courses use two facilitators to bring diversity and continuity.
  • Personal and social voice — clowns are not silent mimes; the work uses language, full emotional range, body, and voice, with a safety rule: never hurt yourself or others, and don’t break anything.

Italian page only: progression through levels 1–3

  • Level 1 — grounds clowning in authenticity, presence, playfulness, and trust; helps overcome fears that block being in the moment.
  • Level 2 — focuses on structure and relationships: awareness of partners, images, stories, characters, and audience; more rigorous improvisation.
  • Level 3 — examines strategies used to avoid mistakes; uses repetition and theatrical technique; moves toward performance.

Main points the content is making

Two-year programme

  • Nose to Nose offers a coherent two-year programme from basics through performance and Social Clowning.
  • Participants can choose their own pace but two years is recommended for integration.
  • Basic training: Clown 1 (one week or 3 weekends), Clown 2, Clown 3, five Performance weekends, and Social Clowning (one week).

Course formats

  • Introductory days (1–2 days) — light introduction to presence and the unexpected.
  • Special theme weekends (2–3 days) — cross-disciplinary research with conflict, biography, ecology, meditation, storytelling, therapy, etc.
  • Ongoing weekends (4–6 per year) — same group, building depth month by month.
  • Intensive residential weeks (~7 hours/day) — full immersion at levels 1–3.
  • Workshops also run outside the UK (Germany, France, Italy, Switzerland, US); groups capped at 12 unless stated otherwise.

Level 1 — Courage to Be

  • Grounds clowning in presence and authenticity; nurtures each person’s unique clown through listening and receptivity.
  • Connects to playful spirit; develops trust in self and life.
  • Inward process focused on overcoming fears that block being in the “Here and Now.”

Level 2 — Embracing the Unexpected

  • Shifts focus to structure and relationship — partner, audience, images, stories, characters.
  • Teaching changes to deepen improvisation and performance technique; new structures bring greater rigour.

Level 3 — The Wisdom of Imperfection

  • Examines strategies for avoiding mistakes; greater theatrical and improvisational technique.
  • Improvs repeated in less comfortable areas; moves toward performance.

Towards Performance

  • Practice improvising before a real audience; includes public performances.
  • Group revisits improvs with insights from each performance.
  • Introduces key principles of Social Clowning.

Social Clowning

  • Draws on Court Jester and Fool traditions.
  • Aims to renew creative processes and bring playful irreverence to relationships and institutions.
  • Practices mirroring social/symbolic references, reclaiming paradox, parodying power, celebrating the heart.
  • Clown holds a mirror to life, puts the human heart at the centre, and speaks truths others cannot.

  • uk/uk-clown-facilitator-training and uk/facilitators-in-training-2012-14 — records of a past facilitator training cohort, not part of the public progression path.

Main points the content is making

Philosophy of applied clowning

  • “Play is wiser than words” — through play we learn about the world, others, self, and communication.
  • Nose to Nose has worked with teachers, social workers, doctors, nurses, and therapists over many years.

Areas of work

  • Social Clowning — events, conferences, meetings (see dedicated offering below).
  • Teacher training.
  • Conflict and communication.
  • Personal development.
  • Special needs (mentioned on Italian stub only in this group).

Social Clowning for organisations

  • Facilitators attend as “normal” guests, observe, then offer 5–10 minute skits summarising key ideas — like Court Jesters.
  • Up to four “reviews” per day event.
  • Brings cheekiness and gentle chaos to break institutional conventions; gets people talking and reframes issues creatively.
  • Example: 2010 Nowhere Academy Creative Dialogue in London (video on original page).

Custom in-house workshops

  • Workshops designed for specific organisations and groups on request.
  • de/interne-workshops lists past clients: Waldorf teacher training (Stuttgart), Goetheanum student conferences, team building (Potsdam), English/German teacher weeks, circus pedagogy (Berlin), refugee circus training, anthroposophical conferences, and more.
  • Recurring themes: intuitive intelligence, courage to be oneself, clown and conflict/communication/trauma.

Burnout prevention (German page)

  • Addresses work pressure, double burdens, routine, miscommunication, and lack of recognition.
  • Uses clowning to discover resources, strengthen perception, and meet professional challenges with lightness and humour.
  • Target groups: doctors, teachers, managers, parents, therapists, social workers, and others under high stress.
  • Workshop format repeats standard Nose to Nose approach (max 12, warm-ups, improvs, discussion, inner clown).

Integration with heart (German page)

  • For companies employing refugees; aims to build empathy and appreciation across language and culture barriers.
  • Uses theatre clowning and improvisation so employees find a shared heartfelt language.
  • Contact: berlin@nosetonose.de

  • A place is confirmed ONLY when Nose to Nose confirms; no deposit taken if the course is full.

Practical expectations

  • Early booking appreciated because of venue commitments.
  • Groups limited to 12; places allocated in order received.

Payment terms and cancellation
